Overview

Abstract

The quadratic cost of attention makes end-to-end training for ultra-high-resolution video generation prohibitively expensive. FreeSwim instead reuses video Diffusion Transformers at their native training scale and extends them to much higher resolutions without any additional training or adaptation.

Our inward sliding-window attention keeps each query token's receptive field aligned with the scale seen during training, improving local fidelity and detail. To prevent the repetition and semantic drift caused by purely local attention, a parallel full-attention branch supplies global semantic guidance through cross-attention override.

A feature cache reuses that global guidance across denoising steps, substantially reducing full 3D attention computation. Across modern video DiTs, FreeSwim produces coherent ultra-high-resolution videos with strong fine detail and competitive efficiency, reaching state-of-the-art VBench performance in a fully training-free setting.

Why FreeSwim

Motivation

Scaling video DiTs beyond their native resolution exposes a fundamental trade-off: global attention preserves structure but misses fine detail, while local windows improve detail at the cost of repetition and semantic inconsistency.

How it works

FreeSwim Framework

Local detail and global structure travel through two coordinated paths, with global features cached and reused for efficient denoising.

1

Preserve native context

Inward windows keep every token's spatial receptive field consistent with the resolution used during training, including boundary tokens.

2

Restore global semantics

A full-attention branch guides the local branch through cross-attention override, reducing repetition and structural inconsistency.

3

Cache and reuse

Global cross-attention features are updated periodically and reused between steps, cutting redundant full 3D attention computation.
